AVIS Declares Its Candidates of Choice: The Only Path Forward for a Fractured Chasmia

By AVIS Editorial Board 9 April 2058, 20:00

The by-election called for Chasmia on 30 June 2058 carries a weight beyond the ordinary churn of Caprican politics. Richard Balls, son of the region's former Prime Minister and its sitting MP since 2056, was murdered by loyalist forces in the Myrati home of Rachel Edelstein-Powell, a killing that closed out the bloody collapse of the Independent Rachelist bloc he had represented. Balls held Chasmia across three party banners over two decades - Liberal, Jasmine Movement, Independent Rachelist - and his death leaves the seat, and the region, without the figure who had defined it for a generation. The Rachelists will not contest the seat they once held. In their place, six names are on the ballot, and for the first time, National Rally.

AVIS does not treat this contest as one among equals, and it will not pretend otherwise. Chasmia is a centre-right region by habit, having returned only the Jasmine Movement or Avenir Caprica since the regions were re-established, and it now faces a field crowded with candidates who offer little beyond recycled relevance or imported extremism. Michael Fairbanks, Avenir Caprica's returning nominee and a former MP for the region, represents a continuation of a right that has already had its say. Izan Canon II arrives as Jasmine Movement's late replacement after Miranda Eiland-Priestly's disqualification under CEC age rules, a substitution born of administrative necessity rather than conviction. Tricky Lindegaard runs nominally as an independent while carrying Rejuvenation Party membership undisclosed on the label. And National Rally's arrival in Chasmia, fronted by Marine Le Pen, is not a debut AVIS will greet with anything but alarm - a party whose politics have no legitimate place in a region already searching for direction after violence.

Against this field, the Social Democratic Alliance has put forward list MP Aldric von Reichel, and AVIS extends him its full and unreserved endorsement. Von Reichel represents the only candidacy in this race grounded in a coherent account of what Chasmia - and Caprica - need after Balls: institutions capable of absorbing shocks like the one that just claimed the seat, a politics that does not retreat into either the stale right or the imported hate of National Rally, and a Republic that takes seriously its obligation to the people history keeps failing. AVIS believes Chasmia's voters deserve to hear plainly: von Reichel is the candidate equal to this moment.

Where AVIS departs from reflexive party loyalty is in its second word. Should Chasmia's voters look past their first preference, Moderate Reform's Patrick Cutter - the sitting Prime Minister, contesting a seat outside his previous base at Mezata - is the only other candidate in this field AVIS can recommend in good conscience. Cutter is no ally of the Alliance's programme, and AVIS will not soften that. He is a Conservative, and the differences between his party's instincts and Social Democratic Alliance's are real. But he has governed as a moderate on the issues that matter most in a region grieving a political murder: agreeable rather than doctrinaire, reachable rather than closed, serious rather than performative. In a race that includes National Rally on one end and a hollowed-out right on the other, that distinction counts for something.

AVIS's message to Chasmia is direct. Vote first for Aldric von Reichel, whose candidacy answers this moment with substance. Where that is not the outcome, Patrick Cutter remains a figure Chasmia can send to the Caprican House without shame. The seat that Ed Balls once held, and his son held after him, deserves better than drift - and better than the politics National Rally now brings to its ballot for the first time.
