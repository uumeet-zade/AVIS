In a dramatic restructuring of the Caprican political landscape, the Democratic Greens of Caprica (DGC) and the Alternative Labor Party (ALP) have formally announced the creation of "GreenLabor," a joint electoral list designed to unify progressive voters. A newly released national poll reveals exactly why the maneuver was necessary: the electorate is fiercely divided, and no single party commands a definitive lead.

According to the latest figures, the Liberal Conservative Alliance (LCA) and the Alternative Labor Party (ALP) are currently tied for the lead, each capturing 21.74 percent of the electorate. Following closely behind is a tie between the Progressive Party (PRO) and the Social Democratic Alliance-Rejuvenation (SDA-REJ) bloc, each holding 17.39 percent. The Democratic Greens of Caprica follow with 13.04 percent, while 8.70 percent of voters report having no voting intentions.

The polling also reveals an interesting shift from the previous election baseline. The ALP lost one respondent who shifted to the DGC, while PRO and SDA-REJ each also saw a voter shift toward the Greens.

The genius of the GreenLabor pact becomes evident when examining the combined statistics. By pooling the ALP and DGC bases, the newly minted GreenLabor coalition commands a formidable 34.78 percent of the electorate. The agreement stipulates that the ALP will remain a steadfast member of the broader Left electoral alliance, maximizing the electoral efficiency of the left while threatening to overwhelm the fractured center-right factions.

Beyond voting intentions, the polling data exposes staggering national polarization regarding the current leadership. President Draqi Arkas faces an intensely divided public; while he maintains a 43.48 percent approval rating (scores of 4 or 5), an identical 43.48 percent of the electorate actively disapproves of his administration, resulting in a net approval rating of precisely zero. This polarization is driven by a massive 39.13 percent of voters handing him the lowest possible rating of 1 out of 5, directly combating the 30.43 percent who gave him a perfect score.

The numbers are even more precarious for Prime Minister Patrick Cutter and the broader Patrick Government. The Prime Minister's national approval has sunk to 34.78 percent, with a 43.48 percent disapproval rate, leaving him underwater with a net approval of -8.70 percent. The Patrick Government as a whole fares even worse, facing a massive 47.83 percent disapproval rate and a net approval of -13.04 percent—the highest net disapproval across all evaluated entities.

Conducted in the days immediately preceding the catastrophic crash of Air Caprica Flight 5735, the poll captures a nation that was already deeply restless and ideologically entrenched. Notably, the poll recorded the final approval metrics for Vice President Alan Bluespan III prior to his death in the Cambria aviation disaster. Bluespan maintained the highest overall approval rating in the nation at 43.48 percent, with only a 26.09 percent disapproval rate, granting him a net positive approval of +17.39 percent—a deeply divided legacy, but one that demonstrated a broader appeal than the President.
