An Air Caprica Boeing 737 Max 9, operating as Flight AC5735 from Montiablo to Alto, has crashed in the mountainous region of Cambria. Cambrian authorities and aviation investigators have confirmed that all 114 passengers and crew members aboard the aircraft were killed upon impact.

Among the victims is former President and current Vice President Alan Bluespan III. His office has yet to issue a formal statement regarding the tragedy.

Preliminary radar data indicates that the aircraft had been cruising normally at approximately 29,000 feet before air traffic controllers observed an extremely rapid and uncontrolled loss of altitude. Radar contact was lost shortly thereafter. There was no reported distress call from the flight crew prior to the crash.

Local residents reported seeing a passenger jet descending at an unusually steep angle toward the mountains, followed by a large column of black smoke rising from a forested valley. Search teams, navigating difficult terrain, reached the primary impact area several hours later. 

Investigators assess that the aircraft struck a steep, forested mountainside at high speed. The scene is one of total devastation. A broad section of the forest has been destroyed around the point of impact, leaving a dark scar visible across the terrain. Trees in the immediate vicinity were snapped, stripped of their branches, or entirely uprooted. Fragments of the aluminum fuselage, cabin materials, and luggage are scattered widely throughout the woodland. At the epicenter of the wreckage, the aircraft carved a massive crater into the exposed earth and shattered rock, leaving very few recognizable sections of the fuselage intact.

Initial emergency operations operated under a search-and-rescue protocol. However, as teams penetrated deeper into the debris field, the catastrophic scale of the impact became evident, leading authorities to confirm the total loss of life.

The passenger manifest reveals that the victims include several foreign nationals, comprising one Maraisellian citizen and two Norwynian citizens. Caprican authorities are currently coordinating with foreign governments before releasing further identities to the public.

Cambrian authorities have established a wide security perimeter around the valley to facilitate recovery operations and allow aviation investigators to access the site. Firefighters, mountain rescue personnel, National Guard units, medical examiners, and police forces are operating from multiple access routes surrounding the crash zone.

The focus of the investigation now turns to locating the aircraft's flight recorders to determine the events that occurred during the final minutes of Flight 5735. Radar observations suggest the aircraft briefly arrested its initial descent before entering a second, fatal plunge. 

Governor Henrik Vasmer has ordered Cambrian flags to be flown at half-mast, describing the disaster as one of the worst aviation accidents in the history of the region.

The crash site remains strictly closed to the public as recovery and investigative operations proceed.
